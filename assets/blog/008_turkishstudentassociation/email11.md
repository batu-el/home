Dear Batu,


Thank you for confirming that you will not contest the board's decision and for your good wishes for the election. Since you've asked that the record be accurate, the record should show what this was actually about.


You frame your removal as a reaction to your "proposing that the community vote on a contested matter." That isn't what happened. The members in question were largely Stanford students who arrived this year, during your own presidency, who have been part of TSA (helping with events and otherwise) and who had been trying to join the mailing list for weeks before they were finally added, which was before the 168-hour cutoff that your own email concedes was within the rules. What you proposed was not that the community decide the election; it was a new procedure under which those legitimate members might not be permitted to vote at all. If the mailing list process failed to include them earlier, that cannot be used now to question their legitimacy once they joined within the deadline. Removing the vote from members who joined within the rules is not protecting the community's voice; it is the opposite, and that is what the board acted on.


You also write that you "deliberately declined to rule" on eligibility. You did rule. In your own email you wrote that the registrations were completed before the freeze and that "on timing alone these subscriptions are not disqualified." Timing is the only test the Constitution provides, and you applied it and found it met. The separate community vote you then created did not decline to decide; it overrode the conclusion you had already reached, through a mechanism that appears nowhere in the Constitution and that the board never approved.


This was also not a sudden decision on our part. I raised these concerns with you directly and repeatedly, by email and by message, before any question of removal arose, warning you that moving outside the Constitution would make the election illegitimate. So did the members whose eligibility your procedure put in doubt. You write that you spent the day deliberating with Alp (a former president with no vote on this board), so you had ample opportunity to hear those objections, and you proceeded anyway. When you nevertheless announced a unilateral procedure affecting voter eligibility, I invoked the removal provision, the remaining board agreed by majority, and your presidency was removed under the Constitution. The removal followed warnings that went unanswered, not the other way around.


There is also a serious inconsistency in your neutrality standard. You excluded Orhun and me from election matters for perceived alignment with candidates, yet you administered the process yourself despite your own clear appearance of alignment with one side. You have recorded a video showing the newly added members' email addresses and passed it to one of the presidential candidates, who posted it to the election group, casting their lawful registration as something to investigate. In the candidates' group, the instant a candidate pushed back by quoting the Constitution, you restricted posting to admins only. Your postscript completes the picture: you write that certain board members "sought to register large numbers of voters to sway the election." That is the framing of one side, not the language of a neutral election administrator. The issue is not merely that you hold that view; it is that you acted on it. You controlled the election's communications and designed an eligibility procedure that could have excluded the very voters whose legitimacy you were questioning. A person taking those actions cannot also claim to be neutrally administering the election.


A note on your P.S.: you suggest that, with your administrator access revoked, you may not be able to present your side fairly, since the moderators now decide which messages reach the list. This email is the answer. It reached the full list because the current board approved it, and your messages will continue to be passed along. We won't do what you did in the candidates' group, where the other side was cut off the moment it pushed back. The fair hearing you're asking for is exactly the one that was denied there.


No hard feelings on my end either. We agree on the destination: the community should decide the election through the constitutional voter list and the constitutional process, not through an eligibility mechanism created after the fact. Going forward, Kuzey will administer the election according to the Constitution, with transparency and appropriate neutral oversight.
