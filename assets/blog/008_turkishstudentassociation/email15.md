Hello everyone,


I would like to share a few observations regarding the voter eligibility issue.


I was present in the current board's WhatsApp group as an informal advisor. I also participated in the discussions we held with the university to seek guidance, so I directly witnessed both the events and the communications that took place behind the scenes. I have no personal stake in this matter and do not plan to take an active role in any future TSA organization. Early in the process, I also stated in writing to both teams running in the election that I would be available to help if they needed my input or assistance.


Now that it is clear I have no personal stake in the outcome, I can comfortably say that Batu did not handle the process alone or in a biased manner. On the contrary, we shared the situation and our bylaws with Stanford's Office of Student Engagement (OSE) both in writing and verbally in a neutral way. We asked for the university's opinion. Kuzey (the current IT Officer) also attended this meeting and can confirm that we described the events transparently without even mentioning any names. If Batu had intended to favor one side, he would not have gone through all of these lengthy efforts before making a decision.


Rather than adding further personal commentary, I believe everyone should evaluate for themselves whether there was any intent or bias involved. The other details I personally witnessed are as follows:


Last Sunday, between 5:29 PM and 7:15 PM, a total of 31 people were batch-enrolled into TSA. Twenty-five of them were enrolled at 5:29 PM, while the others were enrolled after 5:30 PM, the announced election start time. Exactly four minutes after the last individuals were added (at 7:19 PM), the board member who approved these memberships, and who was also a candidate in the election, saved a PDF version of the final email list and shared it in the board WhatsApp group. This board member friend asked whether the people added that day would be allowed to vote and stated that no one added to the mailing list after that time would be accepted.


A Stanford OSE official told Batu that not every edge case must be explicitly addressed in the bylaws and that, in situations not clearly specified, the president (provided they are not running in the election themselves) may exercise ad hoc authority to manage the election. In fact, from the university official's perspective, the last-minute batch enrollment of 25+ individuals by someone running in the election, thereby granting them voting rights, was itself problematic. Despite having the university's support and recommendation not to include these individuals in the election, Batu did not make the decision unilaterally and instead brought the matter to a TSA vote.


My goal is not to cast suspicion on anyone, but rather to help clarify the situation. If there is anything incomplete or inaccurate in what I have written, I encourage the board members to correct it. I also have copies of the relevant communications. I trust that Kuzey will manage the process fairly.


I wish all candidates the best of luck.


Alp Tartıcı

Former TSA President, 2024–2025 term
