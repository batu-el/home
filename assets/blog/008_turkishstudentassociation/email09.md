Onat,


You had the authority to remove me with a board majority, and you exercised it. I won't contest it. My term ends this Sunday regardless, so in practice little changes, but I want the record to be accurate, since these emails will speak for themselves to anyone who reads them.


First, I never made a ruling on eligibility. I deliberately declined to. As the president, I was responsible for administering the election according to the constitution. I could have ruled on the registrations. I did not, because I didn't believe any one person should settle something this consequential to the outcome of the election. Proposing that the community decide was the opposite of a unilateral act.


Second, I wasn't acting alone. I met with Pete Cerneka, Associate Director of Student Organizations, Alp, and Kuzey yesterday afternoon. Following the meeting, when I asked for Kuzey's help, he texted me that he had a deadline and could not help. Alp, our former president, and I continued to discuss the considerations throughout the day and ultimately chose to put the question to the community instead of making a decision.


To be clear about what you have done here: you removed a president for proposing that the community vote on a contested matter. I'm not sure whether that feels like a victory, but your opposition to letting the community decide is, I think, worth sitting with.


I hold no hard feelings. I've invested a lot of time in this community. I'm grateful to have had the chance to serve the Turkish Student Association, and I want it left in good hands. What it needs now is a clean election. The constitution has clear guidance on who runs the elections in the absence of the president. As the new board of three, please run it well.


Good luck,
